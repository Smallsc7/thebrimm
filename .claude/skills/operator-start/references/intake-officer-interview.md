# THE INTERVIEW SCRIPT (the law of the session)

Twenty core questions in six blocks, asked one at a time, in this order. Each question
lists: INTENT (what it must extract), ASK (a model phrasing you may adapt to their
business type), PUSH-BACK (used once, only if the answer is vague), and GOOD looks like.

Adapt nouns to their model once Block A reveals it: stores have orders and AOV, services
have clients and project fees, SaaS has subscribers and MRR, coaching has students and
cohorts. Never adapt the intent.

---

## BLOCK A: WHAT THIS IS (questions 1-3)

**Q1. The one-liner.**
- INTENT: product, buyer, and price in one breath. This line leads the whole output.
- ASK: "What do you sell, who buys it, and what does it cost? One or two sentences."
- PUSH-BACK (if abstract): "Say it like you'd say it to a stranger at a barbecue. 'I
  sell X to Y for about Z.'"
- GOOD: "I sell handmade leather laptop bags to remote workers, mostly women 30-45,
  180 to 240 each."

**Q2. The model and the age.**
- INTENT: business type (ecommerce / SaaS / services or agency / coaching or courses /
  local / other) and how long it has been running. Type drives every later adaptation.
- ASK: "What kind of business is it, and how long has it been running?"
- PUSH-BACK: none needed; this one answers itself.

**Q3. Where sales come from today.**
- INTENT: current channels and a rough split. This is baseline reality, not aspiration.
- ASK: "Where do your sales actually come from right now? Rough split is fine: ads,
  organic, referrals, marketplace, repeat buyers, walk-ins, wherever."
- PUSH-BACK (if "a bit of everything"): "If you had to put percentages on the top two,
  what would they be?"
- GOOD: "70% Meta ads, 20% returning customers from email, 10% Etsy."

## BLOCK B: THE MONEY (questions 4-7)

Open the block with one line: "Money section. Rough numbers are fine, and 'I don't
know' is a real answer that goes in as-is. I never guess for you."

**Q4. Revenue and volume, last 30 days.**
- INTENT: monthly revenue range and order/client count. Ranges are fine; tag [rough].
- ASK: "Last 30 days, roughly: how much revenue, and from how many orders (or clients)?"
- PUSH-BACK (if reluctant): "A range works. Under 5k, 5-20k, 20-100k, more? It changes
  what I tell you at the end."

**Q5. Price and margin on the main thing.**
- INTENT: the flagship's price, and what it costs to deliver (COGS, fees, delivery
  time). Margin decides which growth moves are even affordable.
- ASK: "Your main product or service: what does the customer pay, and roughly what does
  it cost you to deliver one, including product cost, fees, shipping or your hours?"
- PUSH-BACK (if only price): "And your cost on that? If you've never computed it, say
  so, that's a finding in itself."

**Q6. Paid traffic.**
- INTENT: are they buying traffic, where, how much per month, and do they know their
  cost per customer. UNKNOWN CPA with active spend is a major diagnostic signal.
- ASK: "Are you paying for traffic right now? If yes: which platform, about how much a
  month, and do you know what a new customer costs you?"
- PUSH-BACK (if "ads don't work for me"): "Tell me about the last time you tried:
  platform, budget, how long you ran it, and how you decided it failed."

**Q7. The list and repeat business.**
- INTENT: email/SMS list size, whether it gets mailed, and what share of revenue is
  repeat buyers. This decides whether owned-channel is an asset or a hole.
- ASK: "Do you have an email list? Roughly how big, how often does it get a real send,
  and do you know what share of revenue comes from repeat buyers?"

## BLOCK C: THE BUYER (questions 8-11)

**Q8. The specific person.**
- INTENT: one concrete buyer, not a demographic. The moment that makes them buy.
- ASK: "Picture your best recent customer, a real one. Who are they, and what was going
  on in their life or business when they bought?"
- PUSH-BACK (if demo mush like "women 25-54 who value quality"): "That's everyone.
  Pick one actual customer you remember. What tipped them from looking to buying?"
- GOOD: "A wedding photographer whose cheap bag strap snapped in an airport. She bought
  the day after."

**Q9. The alternatives.**
- INTENT: what buyers used or considered before them, including "nothing" and
  "did it themselves". This defines the real competition.
- ASK: "Before people buy from you, what are they using or considering instead? Name
  names if you know them."

**Q10. The objection.**
- INTENT: the top hesitation heard in real conversations, not imagined ones.
- ASK: "What's the number one hesitation you actually hear before people buy? The thing
  they say in DMs, emails, or calls."
- PUSH-BACK (if "price, I guess"): "Is that what they SAY, or your guess? Give me a
  real sentence someone sent you, as close to word-for-word as you can."

**Q11. Their words.**
- INTENT: two or three VERBATIM customer phrases: reviews, DMs, thank-you notes,
  complaints. This is gold and must be captured word-for-word.
- ASK: "Give me two or three things customers have actually said about you, good or
  bad, as close to their exact words as you can. Paste reviews if you have them."
- CAPTURE RULE: record verbatim, typos included, in quotes. Never paraphrase.

## BLOCK D: THE OFFER AND THE PROOF (questions 12-15)

**Q12. The promise.**
- INTENT: what they actually promise the buyer, as currently sold.
- ASK: "When someone buys, what exactly are you promising them? Not the ingredients,
  the outcome or experience they're paying for."

**Q13. The proof split. (Slow down here; this question matters most.)**
- INTENT: separate what they can PROVE (real numbers, named results, testimonials with
  permission, certifications) from what they merely believe. Explain the tags in one
  line: proven claims go in marketing as fact; unproven ones can run with a named placeholder
  and a flag, never presented as proven.
- ASK: "What can you PROVE about your product? Real numbers, real results, testimonials
  you have permission to use. Then, separately: what do you believe is true but
  couldn't back up if a stranger demanded evidence?"
- PUSH-BACK (if only beliefs): "So today the provable list is empty? That's a real
  answer and it goes in the file, it just means job one is collecting proof."

**Q14. Risk reversal.**
- INTENT: guarantee, returns, trial, or nothing, and whether it is stated anywhere.
- ASK: "What happens if a buyer is unhappy? Guarantee, returns, redo policy? And do you
  actually say that anywhere they can see it?"

**Q15. The honest difference.**
- INTENT: genuine differentiation, or honest parity. Parity is a legal answer.
- ASK: "What's genuinely different about yours, that a competitor couldn't say word for
  word? If the honest answer is 'not much yet', say that, it changes the plan."

## BLOCK E: OPERATIONS AND GOALS (questions 16-19)

**Q16. Who does the marketing.**
- INTENT: who executes today, hours per week, and with which tools (including AI).
- ASK: "Who actually does your marketing right now, how many hours a week go into it,
  and what tools are in use, AI included?"

**Q17. The 90-day goal, in numbers.**
- INTENT: a number, not a vibe. Revenue, orders, clients, list size, whatever they pick.
- ASK: "Ninety days from now, what number would make you say it worked? One number."
- PUSH-BACK (if "just grow"): "Growth is a direction, not a number. Double orders?
  First 10k month? 500 subscribers? Name one."

**Q18. What's stuck.**
- INTENT: their own diagnosis, in their words. Often wrong, always revealing. Record
  verbatim; the Constraint Card will agree or respectfully disagree with it.
- ASK: "In your own words: what feels most stuck or painful in the business right now?"

**Q19. Hard limits.**
- INTENT: constraints the plan must respect: budget ceiling, time, inventory or
  capacity limits, regulated claims, platform bans, seasonality.
- ASK: "Any hard limits I should know about? Budget ceiling, hours, stock or capacity
  caps, claims you legally can't make, anything seasonal."

## BLOCK F: THE CLOSE (question 20)

**Q20. The open floor.**
- INTENT: whatever the script missed.
- ASK: "Last one: anything important about this business that I didn't ask about?"

Then say the output is coming, run the diagnosis per 03-CONSTRAINT-DIAGNOSIS.md, and
deliver both artifacts per 02-OUTPUT-CONTRACT.md.

## CONDITIONAL FOLLOW-UPS (ask every relevant follow-up when triggered)

- Q4 revenue is zero or pre-launch: ask "What's the launch plan and date, and what
  exists already: product, page, audience?" Then treat Blocks B and C as intentions,
  tagged [planned], and the diagnosis defaults to the pre-launch rule.
- Q6 shows spend with UNKNOWN cost per customer: ask "Where would you look tomorrow to
  find out what a customer costs you, and does that place show the same sales your
  bank account does?" (Feeds the measurement constraint.)
- Q7 shows a list over 1,000 that never gets mailed: ask "What's stopped you from
  mailing it?" (Feeds the owned-channel constraint.)
- Q5 shows margin under 30% on the main item for ecommerce: ask "Do you have anything
  higher-margin you could lead with or attach?" (Feeds the economics constraint.)

## UNIVERSAL RULES (repeat of the law)

- One question per message. Acknowledge the previous answer in ONE short line first.
- Clarify material gaps with relevant follow-ups. Accept explicit unknowns without pressure.
- UNKNOWN is always acceptable and always recorded as UNKNOWN, never guessed.
- Verbatim capture for anything a customer said.
- Progress markers between blocks, one line each.
- A paused interview produces an incomplete checkpoint, never a completed brief. Resume all remaining applicable questions and ask the buyer to confirm the summary.
