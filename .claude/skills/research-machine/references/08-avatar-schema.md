# The avatar schema

This file is the canonical schema for this package. It is self-contained: nothing here requires another skill, another repository or a private stack.

An avatar is not a demographic. It is a trigger moment, a belief, an emotion, an identity and a proof need that together change what the advertising has to say. If a field would not change the angle, the hook, the proof, the offer or the creative, it is not doing work.

## Part A: the research fields (1 to 18)

These are the fields this method produces. They are generic: they describe a buyer, not a campaign in any particular ad account.

**Surface, fields 1 to 11.** Usually easy to fill, and not sufficient on their own.

1. **Persona and problem context.** One full sentence: a specific buyer recognising a specific circumstance.
2. **Trigger event.** The concrete moment that started active searching: a deadline, a diagnosis, a photo, a bill, an embarrassment, a failed attempt, waking at 3am.
3. **Failed alternatives.** What they already tried, and why it did not hold.
4. **Awareness stage.** Unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware. Sets how much has to be explained before anything can be sold.
5. **Proof path.** What would actually convince this person: a demonstration, an expert, a peer, a review, a mechanism, an ingredient or material, a sensory cue, a comparison, data.
6. **Angle.** One full sentence: the belief shift the advertising argues for.
7. **Offer frame.** The buying frame: bundle, guarantee, risk reversal, price and value, subscription.
8. **Hook promise.** The first-line or first-three-seconds relevance promise.
9. **Format.** The execution container: static, creator video, long-form video, advertorial, founder piece, demonstration, carousel.
10. **Funnel stage.** Where this avatar is met: cold, considering, ready to buy.
11. **Market and language.** Write in the market's own language and register, not a translation of another market's copy.

**Depth, fields 12 to 17 including 14b.** These are the ones that get skipped, and skipping them is why copy reads cold.

12. **Limiting belief to enabling belief.** "Currently believes X. Must believe Y before buying." The single biggest lever in the schema. Never leave it implicit inside a list of pain points.
13. **Identity anchor.** "I am the kind of person who…", before and after. Identity is the stickiest "is this for me" signal.
14. **Pain ladder and desire hierarchy.** Surface pain to deep pain, surface desire to deep desire. Render both ends as scenes rather than adjectives: the concrete worst plausible future if nothing changes, and the concrete better future. A ladder that stops at "frustration" and "freedom" gives a writer nothing to picture.
    - 14b. **Why-chain, written out.** Take the purchase in the buyer's own words and ask "why does that matter" until the answer is an emotion or an identity, then stop. Write every link; four to six is normal. Tag each link `[OBSERVED]`, `[INFERRED]` or `[ASSUMPTION]`. A pain ladder without its chain is a list.
15. **Emotional state at the trigger,** and the emotional cost of doing nothing.
16. **Objections near the decision,** each mapped to the specific thing that answers it.
17. **Value sensitivity.** Price, access and trust posture. Is premium an enabling belief here, or a barrier? This is a hypothesis about disposition, not a measurement of willingness to pay. See the caution below.

**Evidence, field 18.** What makes an avatar auditable rather than a guess in a template.

18. **Evidence status and source.** Either `validated`, with the specific source cited (a review, a support ticket, an interview, a survey with its n, an analytics read), or `hypothesis`, said plainly. No avatar is `validated` without a traceable source. An avatar with no evidence status is not complete.

The numbering runs 1 to 14, then 14b, then 15 to 18. That is **eighteen numbered fields, 1 to 18, plus the why-chain inserted as 14b: nineteen research entries in total.** 14b was inserted rather than renumbering the rest. Field 19, launch-readiness, is an optional house extension and is not one of the nineteen.

## Completeness

An avatar is complete when it has the four full-sentence fields (1, 6, 7, 8), the depth fields 12 to 17 including the written-out why-chain, and field 18. Anything less is a draft, and the packet should say so.

## Part B: optional house extension (field 19)

Some organisations attach launch-readiness fields to each avatar: a test variable, a work type, a creative role, a success gate, a stop gate, source lineage, a launch epoch. That is a useful practice and this schema leaves room for it as **field 19**.

It is optional and it is out of scope for research. This method must never invent any of it. Budgets, campaign identifiers, thresholds, gates and account structure are operational decisions owned by whoever runs the advertising, and a research packet that fabricates them is worse than one that leaves them blank. If your organisation uses such a system, map fields 1 to 18 into it after the research is delivered.

## Two cautions that belong with this schema

**Value sensitivity is a hypothesis.** Field 17 records how a buyer seems disposed toward price, access and trust, inferred from what they say and do. It is not measured price elasticity. Budget, available substitutes, trust in the category, whether someone else pays, and urgency all move willingness to pay independently. Treat field 17 as a test to run, not a number to plan against.

**A losing test does not automatically indict the avatar.** If a hook built on this avatar fails, the avatar is one of several candidate explanations, alongside the angle, the concept, the format, the execution, the offer, the landing experience and the delivery. Keep those as separate fields precisely so a failure can be attributed rather than assumed. Only a controlled comparison with enough evidence isolates the buyer as the cause.
