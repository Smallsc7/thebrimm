# What to do with the packet

## What you just got

One packet every other Machine can run on: the desires your buyers actually have, ranked and in
their words; the objections with what answers each; the alternatives you are really losing to; the
gaps typed by the fix they need; a ranked list of what to test first; and, above all of it, an
honest account of how much evidence sits under each line.

## Hand it to the six

**Put it beside the brief.** In Claude Code or Codex it is already there:
`research/<market>/<run>/PACKET.md`, with `latest` pointing at it, and every Machine you run in
that project finds it. If you run some Machines on claude.ai, save `PACKET.md` and add it to that
Project's knowledge next to the business packet.

**The Copy Machine and the Static Ads Machine** read it through their feed-the-machine skill and skip
every question it already answers; name the packet when you ask them for copy. Their gates treat its `[OBSERVED]` lines as evidence with a link and its
`[ASSUMPTION]` lines as open questions, never as claims.

**The Offer Machine** starts its territories from it:

> Use the offer-machine skill. Run CREATE_FROM_ZERO in AUTOPILOT. Read the research packet at
> [path or attached] first; start the territories from its ranked desires and phrase bank.

**The Meta and Google Ads Machines** take the test matrix as their first candidates:

> Use the meta-machine skill. Read the research packet at [path or attached] first. Build the next
> concept cells from its ranked desires and its test matrix, and tell me which cells its Coverage
> section cannot support yet.

**The Email Machine** takes the phrase bank and the objection table:

> Use the email-machine skill. Read the research packet at [path or attached] first. Use its phrase
> bank for subject and body language and its objection table for the message job of each send.

The Offer, Email, Meta Ads and Google Ads controllers carry this instruction, and the Copy and Static Ads Machines carry it in feed-the-machine, so you do not have to police it:
read the packet first, skip the questions it answers, treat `[OBSERVED]` as evidence with a link
and `[ASSUMPTION]` as an open test. Naming the packet in your prompt just makes it faster.

## The one decision the Machine leaves to you

It recommends a lead desire. You confirm it, because whether your product can honestly satisfy that
desire depends on product knowledge the packet does not fully hold. Everything marked
`[ASSUMPTION]` is a test candidate, never a claim, and anything headed for a customer still passes
the Copy Machine's claims gate.

## Then leave it alone

Once is enough. Re-run on a pivot, on a plateau, or quarterly. A new run never overwrites the old
packet; the comparison between two runs is often worth more than either alone.
