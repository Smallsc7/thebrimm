# Important use note

The Research Machine is a research instrument. Fed a block of facts about a business, it collects
publicly available evidence about a market and produces a packet: coverage notes, a desire map, a
phrase bank, avatars, an objection table, alternatives, typed gaps, a ranked test matrix and open
questions, plus a machine-readable copy and a list of sources. It does not write finished ads,
pages, emails or scripts, it does not decide budgets, and it does not guarantee revenue,
conversion, ranking, traffic, or any other business result.

Public discussion is qualitative evidence, not a survey. It is shaped by who chooses to post, which
communities are visible, what platforms permit to be read, and what people are willing to say
publicly. One quote proves a phrase was used; it never proves how common that view is. Engagement
counts show a conversation is live and cannot size a market. A market read is never a substitute
for talking to your own customers or testing with your own money.

Every finding is labeled as observed, inferred, or an assumption, and every packet opens with its
own coverage and the tier it ran at. Those labels are the product. A recommendation, a ranked test
or a lead desire is a prioritised hypothesis for you to test, never a prediction and never
authorization to spend or to change a live account. All numbers in the example packet and the
references are labeled source claims, illustrations, or hypotheses; your own measured results
outrank them.

Which sources the Machine can reach depends on where you run it and which optional tools you
install. The collector and the services it can use are third-party programs with their own terms
and limits; a source that returns nothing is recorded as unavailable, not estimated. Quotes and
source material belong to the people who wrote them: use them as language evidence, do not
republish them as testimonials for your business, and check the rules of any platform before
reproducing content from it.

Do not paste customer names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, or payment details
into any assistant. Strip identifiers from your own material before sharing it. You are
responsible for the lawfulness of the data you supply, for how you use the output, and for
verifying any claim before you publish it.
